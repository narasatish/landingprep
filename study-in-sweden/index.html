Study in Sweden 2026: The Complete Guide for International Students

Sweden offers one of Europe's widest ranges of English-taught Master's, strong engineering and tech universities, generous scholarships, a 12-month post-study job-search permit and a genuinely clear route to permanent residence. The honest catch: since 2011, non-EU students pay tuition, and living costs are moderate-to-high. This guide walks the whole journey — universities, admission, the residence permit, working, and staying on after graduation.

⚡ Quick answer: Non-EU students pay tuition (≈ SEK 140,000–310,000/yr for most Master's); you must show about SEK 10,314/month for the study residence permit; most Master's are English-taught (IELTS 6.5); apply via universityadmissions.se for the Autumn (August) intake; and after graduating you get a 12-month permit to find work, leading to a clear PR path.

Why study in Sweden?

  • Huge choice of English-taught Master's. You rarely need Swedish at postgraduate level, which makes Sweden very accessible for international students.
  • World-class engineering, tech & innovation. KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Lund University and Chalmers are globally ranked, with deep industry links (Ericsson, Spotify, Volvo). See all top universities in Sweden.
  • Clear post-study & PR pathway — a 12-month job-search permit, then a work permit, then permanent residence within a few years.
  • High quality of life — strong work-life balance, sustainability, and near-universal English fluency.

What it costs (tuition myth busted)

The biggest misconception is that Sweden is free. It was tuition-free until 2011, but non-EU/EEA students now pay tuition — typically SEK 140,000–310,000 per year for most Master's (engineering and computer science at the higher end; business and social sciences often lower). EU/EEA and Swiss students still study free.

Living costs run about SEK 9,000–12,000 per month, higher in Stockholm. Model your full budget with the free cost-of-studying-abroad calculator, and compare Sweden with other destinations in the 2026 funding facts. Good news: scholarships are strong (see below).

Admission & requirements

  • Academic: a recognised bachelor's degree for Master's entry, with relevant background for the programme.
  • English proficiency: typically IELTS 6.5 (no band below 5.5) or TOEFL ~90. Check the exact bar and practise with IELTS for KTH, TOEFL for KTH, and a free IELTS mock test or TOEFL mock test.
  • GRE: generally not required at Swedish universities — a nice cost saving.
  • Applications go through the central portal universityadmissions.se — one application can list up to four programmes, with a single SEK 900 fee.

Money & the student residence permit

Non-EU/EEA students apply for a residence permit for studies (not a visa sticker) through the Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket). You must show funds of about SEK 10,314 per month (2026) for the whole study period, and normally prove your first tuition instalment is paid and that you have comprehensive health insurance. Work out your target total with the free proof-of-funds calculator. Apply as soon as you are admitted — processing can take several weeks to a few months.

Working while you study

Sweden is unusual: there is no fixed legal limit on how many hours international students may work during their studies. That said, full-time study must remain your main activity, and Swedish Master's are intensive — so treat part-time work as a supplement, useful mainly for local experience and living costs, not as your funding plan.

After graduation: work & permanent residence

  • 12-month job-search permit: after a bachelor's or master's you can stay up to 12 months to look for work or start a business (doctoral graduates can get up to 18 months).
  • Work permit → PR: with qualifying employment you move to a work permit; holding one for a few years (while meeting the self-support requirement) opens a clear route to permanent residence.
  • In-demand fields help most — tech, engineering and data roles are strong in Sweden's job market.

Immigration rules and amounts change — always confirm the current details on the official Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket) ↗ before you rely on them.

Scholarships & funding

Because non-EU students pay tuition, scholarships matter. The flagship is the Swedish Institute Scholarships for Global Professionals (SISGP) — highly competitive, covering tuition and living costs — alongside university-specific awards at KTH, Lund and Chalmers. Frequently asked questions

Is studying in Sweden free for international students?
No — this is a common myth. Sweden was tuition-free until 2011, but since then non-EU/EEA students pay tuition, typically about SEK 140,000–310,000 per year for most Master's programmes (engineering and tech at the higher end). Students from the EU/EEA and Switzerland still study free. There are, however, strong scholarships (notably the Swedish Institute Scholarships) that can cover tuition and living costs.
How much money do I need to show for a Swedish student residence permit?
For 2026 the Swedish Migration Agency requires you to show about SEK 10,314 per month for the length of your studies, to cover living costs. The amount can be reduced if your accommodation or food is provided free. You apply for a residence permit for studies (not a visa sticker) through Migrationsverket, and you normally need to have paid your first tuition instalment first.
Can I study in Sweden in English?
Yes, very widely at Master's level — Sweden has one of Europe's largest ranges of English-taught Master's programmes, so you generally don't need Swedish to study there. A typical English requirement is IELTS 6.5 (with no band below 5.5) or equivalent. Bachelor's programmes are more often taught in Swedish.
Can I work while studying in Sweden?
Yes. Unusually, Sweden sets no fixed legal cap on how many hours international students may work during their studies — but your studies must remain your main activity, and full-time study is demanding, so treat any job as a supplement. A part-time job also helps you build local experience and language skills.
Can I stay and work in Sweden after I graduate?
Yes. After completing a bachelor's or master's you can apply for a residence permit of up to 12 months to look for work or start a business in Sweden. If you find qualifying employment you move to a work permit, and holding a work permit for a few years opens a clear route to permanent residence — one of the more straightforward PR paths in Europe.
When should I apply to study in Sweden?
The main intake is Autumn (starts late August), with applications through the central portal universityadmissions.se opening around mid-October and closing around mid-January for the following autumn. A smaller Spring intake (January start) exists for some programmes. There is a one-time application fee of about SEK 900 (waived for scholarship holders and EU/EEA students).
