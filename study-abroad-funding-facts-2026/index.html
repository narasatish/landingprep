Study-Abroad Funding Facts 2026: Proof of Funds & Post-Study Work by Country

A free, citable reference comparing how much money you must show for a student visa, and how long you can work after graduating, across the top study destinations.

⚡ Quick answer: In 2026, proof-of-funds runs from about EUR 10,000/year (Ireland) and EUR 11,904 (Germany Sperrkonto) to CAD 20,635 (Canada GIC) and AUD 29,710 (Australia). The longest post-study work rights are Canada PGWP (up to 3 years) and Australia 485 (2–4 years). Figures are indicative — confirm with the official authority.

Education-loan facts (studying abroad from India)

  • Secured loans (with collateral): typically ~8.5–11% p.a. — the cheapest option.
  • Unsecured / no-collateral loans: typically ~11–15% p.a.; the limit varies by lender, course and university.
  • Moratorium: most education loans don't charge full EMIs during your course plus 6–12 months — paying the simple interest during study cuts the total a lot.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to show for a student visa in 2026?
It varies by country. Germany requires about EUR 11,904 per year in a blocked account; Canada about CAD 20,635 in proof of funds (commonly a GIC); the UK about GBP 1,171–1,529 per month of maintenance for up to 9 months; Australia around AUD 29,710 per year as an indicative benchmark; Ireland about EUR 10,000 per year. The USA has no fixed figure — you prove the first-year cost of attendance for your university. Always confirm the current amount with the official authority before applying.
Which country gives the longest post-study work visa?
Canada's PGWP (up to 3 years), Australia's subclass 485 (2–4 years) and New Zealand's post-study work visa (up to 3 years) are the longest. The UK Graduate Route is 2 years (3 for a PhD) for applications up to the end of 2026, dropping to 18 months from January 2027; US OPT is 12 months plus 24 months for STEM graduates.
Is the German blocked account or Canadian GIC refundable?
Both are your own money. A German Sperrkonto releases roughly EUR 992 to you each month once you arrive and register; the Canadian GIC is returned to you in instalments over your first year. Neither is a fee — they are proof of funds.
Do these funding figures change?
Yes — most countries update proof-of-funds amounts every year, and exchange rates move. Treat the figures here as indicative 2026 values and confirm the exact current requirement on the official immigration website before you apply.
