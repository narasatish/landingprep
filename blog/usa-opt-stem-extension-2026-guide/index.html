USA Optional Practical Training (OPT) & STEM Extension 2026: Complete Guide

Optional Practical Training (OPT) allows F-1 visa graduates to work legally in the USA for 12 months after graduation. International students in STEM fields (Science, Technology, Engineering, Mathematics) qualify for an additional 24-month extension, totaling up to 36 months of work authorization.

OPT is the primary pathway for Indian graduates to transition from student status to employment-based immigration (H-1B, EB-3, L-1 visas).

Who Qualifies for OPT in 2026?

You are eligible for OPT if you meet all of the following criteria:

🔑
2024 policy update: USCIS tightened OPT eligibility—you must apply within 60 days of graduation if you've left the USA. If you're still in the US on F-1 status, you have more flexibility. Confirm current rules on USCIS.gov.
  • Current or recent F-1 visa holder: Maintain or recently maintained F-1 status while studying
  • Completed program: Graduated with a degree (bachelor's, master's, or doctorate) or completed a full academic year and maintain F-1 status
  • USCIS authorization: Your degree was completed within the past 60 days (new 2024 rule; previously 12 months), OR you maintain valid F-1 status
  • STEM vs. Non-STEM: All F-1 graduates qualify for 12-month OPT; STEM graduates qualify for an additional 24-month extension
  • Employment intent: Plan to work in a position related to your field of study

OPT Duration: Base 12 Months + STEM Extension Up to 24 Months

OPT work authorization is split into two phases:

  • Initial 12-month OPT: Available to all F-1 graduates in any field; must be related to field of study
  • STEM Extension (24 months): Available only if your degree is in a STEM field listed by USCIS; adds 24 additional months for total of 36 months
  • Gap in employment: You can have up to 90 days of unemployment total on OPT; any time over 90 days may violate your OPT and require repatriation
  • Multiple OPT periods: Can have pre-completion OPT (before graduation, 1 month max) and post-completion OPT (12 months after graduation)

STEM Fields & OPT Extension Eligibility

STEM Extension eligibility is based on your degree major, NOT your job title. Check if your degree qualifies for STEM extension:

  • STEM degree examples: Computer Science, Engineering (all types), Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Data Science, Cyber Security, Information Security
  • Borderline fields (check USCIS list): Business with CS focus, Finance with quant focus—only qualify if your specific degree is STEM-listed
  • Non-STEM degrees (no extension): Business Administration, MBA, Liberal Arts, Social Sciences, Economics, Law, most humanities
  • Verification: Search USCIS's official STEM degree list on uscis.gov using your exact degree name; if unsure, consult your university's international student office

OPT Application Process: Step-by-Step Timeline

Apply for OPT 120–90 days before your program end date to avoid employment gaps:

⚠️
Processing delays: OPT approval can take 2–4 months (sometimes 6+ months if additional evidence requested). Apply as early as possible—120 days before graduation—to avoid employment start delays.
  1. Step 1 (120–90 days before graduation): Obtain official recommendation letter from your university's international student office (OPT recommendation)
  2. Step 2: Complete the Form I-765 (Application for Employment Authorization Document) available on USCIS.gov
  3. Step 3: Gather required documents: Form I-765, passport photocopy, copy of F-1 visa or I-94 record, university OPT recommendation letter, recent passport photo
  4. Step 4: Calculate the fee: $410 (as of 2026; confirm on USCIS.gov). Make payable to 'U.S. Department of Homeland Security'
  5. Step 5: Choose filing location: mail to your designated USCIS service center (based on your university location) or use USCIS online filing portal (ELIS) if available in your jurisdiction
  6. Step 6: Track application status on USCIS.gov using your receipt number (sent to you after submission)
  7. Step 7: Receive Employment Authorization Document (EAD/OPT card) in mail (typically 2–4 months; expedited processing available for additional fee)
  8. Step 8: Begin employment within 60 days of EAD receipt. Employer must verify your authorization via E-Verify system

E-Verify Requirement & Employer Responsibility

All employers sponsoring OPT employees must verify work authorization through E-Verify, the federal system that confirms you're authorized to work in the USA.

  • E-Verify: Your employer submits your I-9 documents to E-Verify within 3 days of hire. System verifies your name, birth date, Social Security Number against government databases
  • Employer compliance: Most US employers use E-Verify; if they don't, they must use Form I-9 verification but are not compliant with federal regulations
  • Your responsibility: Ensure employer has your correct legal name, birth date, passport number, and Social Security Number (obtain an ITIN from IRS if needed)
  • E-Verify matches: 99% of OPT employees pass E-Verify on first try. If there's a mismatch, work with your employer and USCIS to resolve it within 8 business days

OPT Cap-Gap & H-1B Bridge Visa

If your OPT expires before your H-1B visa begins (typically October 1), you can work on cap-gap authority—transitional work authorization while awaiting H-1B approval.

  • Cap-Gap eligibility: You must have an H-1B petition filed by April 1 for October 1 H-1B start date
  • Gap period: OPT typically expires 12 months after start date. If H-1B lottery doesn't select you until July–August, you have a 2–3 month gap
  • Cap-Gap authorization: USCIS grants automatic extension on your current EAD (same OPT card) through the H-1B start date (October 1)
  • No action required: Cap-Gap is automatic if H-1B petition is pending; no additional application needed
  • Employer continuation: Continue working for the same employer or switch employers if new employer files an H-1B petition before your OPT expiration

Transitioning from OPT to H-1B Visa Sponsorship

After OPT, most Indian graduates transition to H-1B visa sponsorship for continued employment in the USA. H-1B is an employment-based visa valid for 3 years (extendable to 6 years).

OPT → H-1B timeline: - Year 1 (OPT): Work on initial 12-month OPT, gain US work experience - STEM Extension (optional): Apply for 24-month STEM extension if eligible; work until year 3 - H-1B filing window: Employer files H-1B petition in March–April for October 1 start date - H-1B approval: USCIS processes petition by September; cap-gap bridges any gap until October - H-1B visa stamp: Consular processing in India or visa interview (if outside USA) for H-1B visa stamp

Maintaining F-1 Status While on OPT

While working on OPT, you must maintain compliance with F-1 visa requirements to preserve your status and H-1B eligibility:

  • Valid passport: Keep your passport valid throughout OPT (recommended to have 6+ months validity beyond OPT end date)
  • I-20 document: Maintain your I-20 from your university (even while on OPT); some employers require it for employment verification
  • Address reporting: Update SEVP (Student and Exchange Visitor Program) with any address changes within 10 days
  • No breaking the law: Any criminal conviction, overstay, or unauthorized employment voids your F-1 status and disqualifies you from H-1B sponsorship
  • Return/transfer: If you leave the USA before OPT expires, you may lose remaining OPT authorization; confirm with your DSO (Designated School Official) before departing

Frequently asked questions

What is OPT for F-1 students in the USA?
**OPT (Optional Practical Training)** is work authorization for F-1 graduates to work in the USA for 12 months after graduation. STEM graduates can extend for an additional 24 months (total 36 months).
How long can I work on OPT in the USA?
**12 months of initial OPT** for all F-1 graduates. **STEM graduates: up to 36 months total** (12 months initial + 24-month extension). Non-STEM graduates are limited to 12 months with no extension.
What is the STEM extension for OPT?
STEM extension is an **additional 24-month work authorization** available only to graduates in STEM fields (Computer Science, Engineering, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, etc.). Eligibility is based on your degree major, not your job title.
How do I apply for OPT?
File **Form I-765 (Application for Employment Authorization Document)** with USCIS 120–90 days before graduation. Fee is **$410**. Processing takes 2–4 months. Submit with university OPT recommendation letter.
Do I need a job offer to apply for OPT?
No, OPT does NOT require a job offer. You can apply immediately after graduation and search for a job during OPT validity. However, your job must be related to your field of study.
What is cap-gap and how does it work?
**Cap-gap** is automatic work authorization extension while your H-1B visa petition is pending. If your OPT expires before H-1B approval (July–September), USCIS automatically extends your EAD through October 1 H-1B start date.
Do employers need to do E-Verify for OPT employees?
Yes, employers **must verify your work authorization through E-Verify** within 3 days of hire. Most US employers use E-Verify; they submit your I-9 documents, and the system confirms you're authorized to work.
Can I change employers while on OPT?
Yes, you can **change employers anytime on OPT** with no restrictions. Your OPT authorization is not tied to a single employer (unlike H-1B, which is employer-specific). New employer must complete E-Verify.
