How to Get PR in Canada After Study: Express Entry Basics & Points System

The most common route to Canada PR for international students — Express Entry, the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) and how to accumulate points.

▶ Free College Predictor & study-abroad tools

Express Entry is the main path

Most international students in Canada apply for PR through Express Entry, which manages applications for three federal economic-immigration programmes: Federal Skilled Worker (FSW), Federal Skilled Trades (FST), and Canadian Experience Class (CEC). As a recent graduate, CEC is usually your fastest route.

CRS points explained

Your eligibility is ranked by Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) points (0–1,200 total). Points come from age, education, English/French ability, work experience, and having a provincial job offer. A recent Indian student graduate with a Canadian postgraduation work permit (PGWP), a local job offer, and IELTS 7.0+ typically scores 400–450 CRS points.

The language score lever

A high IELTS or CELPIP score is the single fastest way to improve your CRS. Each test-band increment adds 25–50 points. IELTS 8.0 (vs 7.0) adds significant points. Practising English for CRS is worth it.

Provincial Nominee Programme (PNP)

Many provinces (Ontario, BC, Alberta) have their own PNP streams for recent graduates. These often have lower CRS cutoffs and faster processing. If your province of study has a PNP, check if you're eligible — it can be faster than federal Express Entry.

How much work experience you need

The Canadian Experience Class requires 1 year of skilled work experience (full-time) in the past 3 years. Your PGWP allows you to work, so after one year on your PGWP, you become eligible to apply for PR. This is why the PGWP length matters.

Timeline to PR

Overall timeline: 2 years study + 1 year PGWP work + 3–6 months Express Entry processing = roughly 3–4 years from start of study to approved PR.
