TOEFL vs. Duolingo English Test: Format, Difficulty, Cost, Acceptance (2026)

TOEFL vs. Duolingo: Quick Comparison

Both TOEFL iBT and Duolingo English Test (DET) are accepted by universities worldwide, but they differ in format, cost, difficulty, and what they measure. Neither is 'easier'—they test different skills. Choose based on your strengths and university requirements.

The headline: - TOEFL is the gold standard; accepted universally, especially in the US & UK. - Duolingo is newer, cheaper, faster, and increasingly accepted—but not everywhere. - TOEFL heavily tests lecture comprehension and formal writing. - Duolingo tests reading fluency, conversation, and quick thinking.

If you're unsure which test your university requires, check their admissions website first. Many universities accept both; some prefer TOEFL; a few only accept TOEFL.

🔑
As of 2026, Duolingo English Test is accepted by 3,000+ universities globally, including Harvard, MIT, Stanford, and most UK Russell Group schools. However, a handful of prestigious schools (Yale, Princeton, Cambridge) still require TOEFL or IELTS. Always verify with your target university before committing to a test.

TOEFL: Strengths & Ideal Test-Taker

TOEFL Strengths: - Universal acceptance: Accepted by 10,000+ universities in the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and beyond. - Clear section scores: Each skill is measured separately (Reading 0–30, Listening 0–30, Speaking 0–30, Writing 0–30). Universities see exactly where you're strong/weak. - Academic focus: Tests listening to lectures, reading academic texts, and writing essays—skills directly needed in college. - Stable reputation: TOEFL has been the standard for 40+ years. No university will question your TOEFL score. - Detailed rubrics: TOEFL's grading is transparent; you can study to the rubric.

TOEFL is ideal if: - You're applying to a top-50 US university (nearly all require 100+). - You're strong in reading and listening but weaker in speaking (TOEFL's integrated tasks let you prepare in advance). - You prefer a structured test with clear section scores. - Your target school specifically requires TOEFL.

ℹ️
All TOEFL scores are reported to your selected universities automatically at registration. You cannot choose which schools see your score, and you cannot 'cancel' a score after seeing it. (You can retake and report a new score, but the old one stays on your record.)

Duolingo English Test: Strengths & Ideal Test-Taker

Duolingo Strengths: - Dramatically cheaper: $49 vs. TOEFL's $230–280. Over 5x cheaper. - Fast delivery: Take the test at home, get results in 1–2 days (vs. TOEFL's 3–9 days). - Convenience: No test center required. Take it on any computer with a webcam. - Rising acceptance: Now accepted by 3,000+ universities, including Stanford, MIT, Harvard, and most UK schools. - Adaptive difficulty: The test adjusts difficulty based on your answers, so it doesn't feel repetitive. - No section scores, but detailed report: You get an overall 10–160 score + a detailed report showing reading, listening, speaking, writing proficiency.

Duolingo is ideal if: - Cost is a major factor (international students with tight budgets). - You want results quickly (applying with a deadline 2–3 weeks away). - You're a fluent speaker and strong reader but weaker at formal essay writing. - You're applying to schools that explicitly accept Duolingo (check their website). - You prefer a conversational, adaptive test over a rigid, proctored environment.

⚠️
A small but real group of top universities (Yale, Princeton, University of Cambridge, Oxford) do NOT accept Duolingo English Test. If you're targeting these schools, take TOEFL. Check your university's admissions page; it will state 'TOEFL' or 'TOEFL/IELTS/Duolingo accepted.'

Difficulty Comparison: Which Is Harder?

This is subjective and depends on your strengths, but here's the consensus:

TOEFL is harder if: - You struggle with lecture comprehension (especially accents and fast speech in Listening). - You're uncomfortable writing 300+ words under time pressure. - You're not confident with formal English (TOEFL uses academic, formal vocabulary and tone). - You need to balance 4 sections; one weak section tanks your overall score.

Duolingo is harder if: - You're not a fast reader (the test is packed with reading and very time-pressured). - You're not good at thinking on your feet (real-time conversation; no prep time like TOEFL Speaking). - You're a non-native speaker who relies on writing time to collect your thoughts. - You struggle with tech (Duolingo is adaptive and glitchy on older computers).

The Real Answer: Neither test is 'easier.' They test different skills. - TOEFL rewards preparation: You can study the rubric, practice with official materials, and refine your essays. If you prepare for 12 weeks, you'll score 100+. - Duolingo rewards natural fluency: You cannot cram Duolingo like TOEFL. It tests conversational English and quick thinking. If you're already fluent, you'll score 120+ easily. If you're non-fluent, no amount of prep helps—you'll see what level you actually are.

Acceptance Rates: Which Universities Prefer Which?

TOEFL is universally accepted: - 10,000+ universities in 150+ countries. - 100% of top 50 US universities accept TOEFL. - 100% of Russell Group (UK) universities accept TOEFL (most also accept Duolingo, but list TOEFL first). - Required by many universities' official policy.

Duolingo is increasingly accepted but with gaps: - 3,000+ universities globally. - All major US universities (except Yale, Princeton, Stanford historically required TOEFL, but now most accept Duolingo). - Most UK universities (but Oxford and Cambridge prefer TOEFL; check their sites). - Rarely the ONLY accepted test; usually alongside TOEFL/IELTS.

Universities that do NOT accept Duolingo (2026): - Yale University - Princeton University - University of Cambridge - University of Oxford (technically accepts it, but not prominently advertised) - A handful of smaller, older universities in the US & UK

The rule: If your target university's website says 'TOEFL/IELTS accepted,' Duolingo is probably also accepted. If it says 'TOEFL required,' do not assume Duolingo works; email admissions.

For most Indian students aiming for US/UK universities, TOEFL is the safer bet. Duolingo works for 90%+ of universities, but the 10% gap includes some elite schools.

🔑
MIT, Stanford, Harvard, and most top-50 US universities now accept Duolingo English Test. This is a major shift from 2023. However, verify on each school's official admissions page—policies change.

Duolingo Scoring Explained

Duolingo uses a 10–160 scale (vs. TOEFL's 0–120). The score reflects overall proficiency across all skills.

What universities expect: - 160 (perfect): Fluent; exceeds expectations. Rare (~1% of test-takers). - 140–155: Strong proficiency. Competitive for top universities. - 125–139: Intermediate-advanced. Meets requirements for most universities. - 100–124: Intermediate. Acceptable for many universities; below target for elite schools. - Below 100: Elementary/beginner. Usually not accepted for university.

Equivalence (rough): - Duolingo 120 ≈ TOEFL 90–95 - Duolingo 130 ≈ TOEFL 100 - Duolingo 140 ≈ TOEFL 105–110 - Duolingo 150+ ≈ TOEFL 115+

Duolingo's scale is somewhat inflated compared to TOEFL (a Duolingo 140 might be closer to TOEFL 100 in practice), so universities often require Duolingo 130+ for programs where TOEFL 100 would suffice.

ℹ️
Duolingo does not provide sub-scores (reading, listening, speaking, writing breakdowns). You get one overall score + a detailed report. Universities cannot see where your weaknesses are, so there's no partial credit for strong reading if your speaking is weak.

Test-Day Experience: What to Expect

TOEFL Test-Day: - Arrive 30 min early at an official test center. - Leave your phone, bag, and all belongings outside the testing room. - You get 1 pencil and scratch paper (provided). - 3 hours total: Reading (54–72 min) → Listening (41–57 min) → 10-min break → Speaking (17 min) → Writing (50 min). - Proctors monitor via camera; any suspicious behavior can void your score. - Strict rules: no bathroom breaks during sections, no water, no talking. - After the test, you can cancel or keep your score on the spot.

Duolingo Test-Day: - Sit at your desk with a computer/phone. - Install Duolingo's proctoring app; it monitors via webcam (you must show your face and surroundings). - ~1 hour total. No break between sections. - Test includes: reading, listening, conversation, writing. - More relaxed environment, but the app can be glitchy (audio/video drops are common; Duolingo refunds if tech fails). - You see your score immediately after the test.

Comfort factor: Duolingo is less stressful (at home, no long wait). TOEFL is more formal (official test center, proctors watching).

How to Choose: Decision Framework

Use this flowchart to decide which test is right for you:

Step 1: Check your target university's policy. - Does it require TOEFL only? → Take TOEFL. - Does it accept TOEFL/IELTS/Duolingo? → Either is fine; move to Step 2. - Does it require Duolingo? → Take Duolingo (rare, but growing).

Step 2: What's your deadline? - Need results in 2 weeks? → Duolingo (1–2 days vs. TOEFL's 3–9 days). - Have 2+ months? → Either; prioritize quality.

Step 3: What's your budget? - Tight budget (<$100)? → Duolingo ($49). - Can spend $250–400? → TOEFL (better long-term reputation).

Step 4: What are your English strengths? - Strong listener + reader, weaker speaker? → TOEFL (can prep for Speaking). - Already fluent, native-like conversationalist? → Duolingo (rewards natural fluency). - Balanced but deliberate (not quick-thinking)? → TOEFL (more time to prepare answers). - Quick-thinking, conversational, creative? → Duolingo (real-time, adaptive).

Step 5: Risk tolerance? - Want the 'safe' option that all universities recognize? → TOEFL. - Willing to gamble on Duolingo if it's cheaper/faster? → Duolingo.

Common decision: Indian students applying to US/UK universities: TOEFL is safer (universal acceptance + clear scoring). But if you're applying to 20+ universities and cost matters, take Duolingo first (cheap, fast) and retake TOEFL only if needed.

  1. Open your target university's admissions page and search 'English language requirements' or 'TOEFL.'
  2. Note: Does it say 'TOEFL' only, or 'TOEFL/IELTS/Duolingo'?
  3. If Duolingo is accepted, note the required score (often 130–140 for competitive programs).
  4. If only TOEFL, note the required score (usually 90–100 for master's, 100+ for top schools).
  5. Cross-check 5–10 of your target schools. If all accept Duolingo, take Duolingo. If 1+ requires TOEFL, take TOEFL.

Can You Take Both? Should You?

Yes, you can take both TOEFL and Duolingo. Some students do this as insurance.

Reasons to take both: - Duolingo is so cheap ($49) that taking it as a 'backup' makes sense. - If you score poorly on one, the other might be accepted by your backup schools. - Some universities ask for multiple test scores; having both strengthens your application. - TOEFL section scores show detailed strengths; Duolingo shows overall fluency. Together, they paint a complete picture.

Strategy: 1. Take Duolingo first (cheap, quick). If you score 130+, you're done (accepted by most universities). 2. If Duolingo is below 130, or you're targeting Yale/Princeton/Cambridge, take TOEFL. 3. Total cost: $49 + $230 = $279 (less than one TOEFL retake).

Caution: - Universities do not require multiple English tests. Taking both is overkill for most programs. - Admissions committees see multiple scores and may question why (e.g., did the first score disappoint?). - Your time is limited; 12 weeks of prep for one test is better than 6 weeks each for two tests.

💡
If you're borderline between TOEFL and Duolingo, take Duolingo first. It costs 1/5 the price and gives you results in 1–2 days. If you score well, submit it and save $230. If you don't, you have time to prep for TOEFL.

Frequently asked questions

Is Duolingo English Test accepted by Harvard, MIT, and Stanford?
Yes, as of 2026. Harvard, MIT, Stanford, and most top-50 US universities accept Duolingo English Test. However, verify on their official admissions page because policies change. Yale and Princeton were slower to adopt; confirm before assuming.
What's the minimum Duolingo score universities want?
Most US universities want Duolingo 120+. Top universities (top 50) want 130+. UK universities want 120–130 depending on the program. Compare to your target school's TOEFL requirement and ask 'What Duolingo equivalent?' on their admissions chat.
Can I retake TOEFL or Duolingo if I don't like my score?
Yes. TOEFL allows 5 retakes per calendar year (you can take it again after 3 days). Duolingo has no official limit; you can retake whenever. However, all scores are reported to universities; you cannot hide a low score. Universities see every attempt, so retake only if you're confident you'll improve.
How much harder is TOEFL than Duolingo?
Duolingo is not 'easier'; it's different. TOEFL is harder if you're weak in listening/formal writing. Duolingo is harder if you can't think on your feet. Most non-natives find Duolingo slightly easier because it tests conversational English, not academic essays.
Which test should I take if I'm already fluent in English?
Duolingo. Fluent speakers score 140+ on Duolingo easily (15 min of practice) but may score 110–115 on TOEFL (which tests formal writing). If you're a native or near-native speaker, Duolingo rewards you; TOEFL doesn't.
Can I use my Duolingo app score as proof of English? Or do I need the official test score?
No. The Duolingo app (free version) does not issue official test scores. You must take the official **Duolingo English Test** (paid, proctored) to get a certificate. The app is for practice only.
What if my university doesn't list Duolingo on their website but accepts it anyway?
Email admissions. Ask explicitly: 'Do you accept Duolingo English Test scores?' Most universities now do, even if they don't advertise it. Email response takes 1–2 days; it's worth checking.
If I take TOEFL and score 95, is it better to retake or take Duolingo?
Depends on your target score. If you need 100+, retake TOEFL (you know the test now; 3–4 more weeks of targeted prep should get you to 100). If 95 is acceptable, submit it. Do NOT take both unless you're targeting Yale/Princeton specifically.
Are TOEFL and Duolingo scores weighted differently by universities?
No official weighting; both are equivalent. However, TOEFL is more recognized historically, so some elite universities may give a slight preference to TOEFL. In practice, a Duolingo 140 and a TOEFL 105 are equally competitive for most schools. Check your university's policy.
