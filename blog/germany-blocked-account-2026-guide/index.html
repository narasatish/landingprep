What Is a German Blocked Account (Sperrkonto)?

A Sperrkonto (literally 'blocked account') is a special German bank account that proves you have sufficient funds to support yourself during your studies in Germany. German immigration requires you to show EUR 11,904 per year (or EUR 992 per month as of 2025–26) before issuing a student visa.

This amount is NOT a deposit that stays locked forever. Instead, you make monthly withdrawals (typically EUR 992/month) once you've obtained your residence permit and registered at a German university. The remaining balance is released after you complete your degree or leave Germany.

The blocked account exists to prevent student visa abuse and ensure you can afford tuition, accommodation, and living expenses without becoming a financial burden on the German state.

Think of it as a trust account: the German government trusts you have the funds, you trust the account provider will release them monthly, and the account provider trusts you'll withdraw responsibly. It's not a savings account—it's proof of financial stability, pure and simple.

2026 Blocked Account Amount & Monthly Withdrawal

The German government updates the required amount annually. For 2025–26, the figure is EUR 11,904 per year, calculated as roughly EUR 992 per month.

💰
EUR 11,904/year = ~₹10 lakh (INR) at current rates. Confirm the exact 2026 figure on the German embassy website in India before applying.
  • Amount covers tuition, rent, food, transport, insurance, and miscellaneous expenses.
  • If you are staying for 2 years (bachelor's final semester + master's 1st year), required amount is ~EUR 23,808.
  • Non-EU students must provide proof of funds before applying for a student residence permit (Aufenthaltserlaubnis).
  • Spouses or dependents each require an additional EUR 5,952 for the same period.

Step-by-Step: Opening a Sperrkonto

We'll walk through opening with Fintiba (most popular) and Expatrio (fastest). The process is almost identical for all platforms.

  1. Visit Fintiba.de or Expatrio.de and click 'Open Account' or 'For Students'.
  2. Create an account with your email, full name (as in passport), and date of birth.
  3. Select 'Germany' as destination country, your university, and course start date.
  4. Choose how much to deposit (EUR 11,904 for 1 year; EUR 23,808 for 2 years, etc.).
  5. Provide your passport scan (photo + info page), proof of admission (acceptance letter or Zulassungsbescheid), and proof of transfer origin (if required by your bank).
  6. Make a one-time wire transfer from your Indian bank account to the provider's account in India or Germany. Watch for 'NEFT' or 'SWIFT' codes—use the international wire option.
  7. Account opens within 3–7 days; you receive a statement and account number via email.
  8. Use this account number and statement in your German visa application (apply at the German embassy in New Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, or Kolkata).
  9. After receiving your visa and registering at your university, log into your blocked account and set up monthly withdrawals (EUR 992 or as permitted by the provider).
  10. Funds are released incrementally; balance is returned after you leave Germany.

Funding Sources: Where to Get EUR 11,904

Indian parents or students often ask: do I have to show my own money, or can my parents fund it? The good news: multiple sources of funding are accepted by German immigration. Here's a breakdown:

  • Parents' funds: Yes, parents can deposit into the Sperrkonto. The account is in your name, but the source is your family. Provide a family relation affidavit if needed. This is the most common approach for Indian students.
  • Education loan: Some Indian lenders (HDFC Credila, Avanse, Auxilo) offer education loans for Germany, covering the Sperrkonto amount. Loan approved? Use it to fund the Sperrkonto. The lender disburses directly, and you're not left with a large debt before you even start university. See our education loan guide.
  • Scholarship / part-scholarship: If you've received a partial scholarship (e.g., EUR 5,000/year), you still need to show the remaining EUR 6,904 in a Sperrkonto. Scholarships from German universities are rare for undergraduates, but common for Master's students. Check your university's financial aid office.
  • Part-time student job in Germany: You can work up to 120 full days or 240 half days per year while studying (roughly 20 hours/week). This income can supplement your Sperrkonto gradually over time, but you MUST still show the full EUR 11,904 upfront before your visa is approved.
  • NRI parent in Germany: If a parent or relative is already working in Germany (e.g., on a work visa or PR), they can sponsor your Sperrkonto. This is fastest because no wire transfer is needed—funds stay in Germany.

From Blocked Account to Monthly Withdrawals: Unblocking Process

Once in Germany, your account transitions from 'blocked' to 'accessible.' Here's what happens:

  1. Receive your German residence permit (Aufenthaltserlaubnis) from local immigration (Ausländerbehörde).
  2. Register at the town hall (Rathaus) and receive your Meldebestätigung (registration certificate).
  3. Log into your Fintiba/Expatrio app and submit your residence permit + university enrollment documents.
  4. Confirm your monthly withdrawal amount (usually EUR 992, but you can request adjustments).
  5. Set up automatic monthly transfers to your spending account (Girokonto), or withdraw via ATM.
  6. Each month, EUR 992 (or your agreed amount) is deducted from the blocked balance and made available.
  7. When your account balance reaches zero (after ~12 months), it's closed automatically.
  8. If you leave Germany early, the remaining balance is refunded (minus any fees).

Common Mistakes & How to Avoid Them

Many Indian students run into avoidable pitfalls that delay or derail their visa. Here's what NOT to do:

  • Transferring to the wrong account: Double-check bank details (IBAN, BIC, account holder name) with the provider multiple times. A single typo in the IBAN will cause the wire to bounce, delaying your account opening by 2–3 weeks. Copy-paste the IBAN directly from the official email.
  • Delaying the deposit: Apply for the Sperrkonto 2–3 months before your visa interview. Visa officers want to see a recently opened account with proof of funds—not a plan to open one 'soon.' A 6-month-old account looks suspicious (where has the money been?).
  • Not keeping documentation: The German embassy may ask for the account statement, letter from the bank, and proof of transfer. Keep PDFs in your email and printed copies in your visa file folder. Embassies often lose documents; have backups.
  • Forgetting to unblock after arrival: Many students arrive in Germany and never contact Fintiba/Expatrio to unlock monthly disbursements. You get stuck waiting weeks without living money. Do this within 1–2 weeks of registration.
  • Mixing up Sperrkonto with a regular savings account: A regular Indian savings account will NOT work for a German visa application. You MUST use a designated Sperrkonto provider (Fintiba, Expatrio, etc.). Visa officers will reject regular bank statements.
  • Opening an account after submitting your visa application: Your application will be delayed or rejected if you don't show a Sperrkonto statement before the interview. Open it BEFORE you apply.
  • Not informing the provider of your university change: If you change universities after opening the Sperrkonto, update your account. Mismatch between account university and visa university triggers extra scrutiny.

Costs & Timeline: What to Budget

Beyond the EUR 11,904 itself, here are other costs to consider:

  • Wire transfer fee: SWIFT wire from your Indian bank to Fintiba = ₹500–₹2,000. Wise is often cheaper (₹200–₹400).
  • Exchange rate loss: INR-to-EUR conversion incurs a 1–3% margin. Budget 2.5% extra.
  • Account maintenance after opening: Fintiba and Expatrio are free. Deutsche Bank charges ~EUR 5–10/month.
  • Visa processing: German student residence permit is FREE, but you must book a visa appointment (no fee) at the embassy.
  • Timeline: Open the Sperrkonto NOW if you're applying for the next intake (Sept/Oct 2026). Aim to complete all steps 60 days before your visa interview.
  • Total cost of funds + transfers ≈ EUR 11,904 + EUR 40–150 (fees). Plan for ~EUR 12,000 all-in.

Next Steps After Sperrkonto Approval

Once your blocked account is set up, here's what comes next:

  • Compile your visa application file: passport, Sperrkonto statement, acceptance letter, SOP, academic transcripts, English language test (TOEFL/IELTS).
  • Book a visa appointment at the German embassy (allow 4–8 weeks for processing).
  • Attend the interview with all documents. The officer will ask about your funds—tell them the Sperrkonto is locked until you arrive.
  • Receive your visa (valid for 90 days). Use this time to arrange accommodation in Germany (check HousingAnywhere, WG-Gesucht, or university housing).
  • Arrive in Germany, register at the town hall, and submit your residence permit documents to unlock monthly withdrawals.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the German blocked account amount in 2026?
The required amount for 2025–26 is **EUR 11,904 per year** (EUR 992/month). Confirm the exact figure for 2026 on the German embassy website, as it may increase slightly with inflation.
Which Sperrkonto provider is best for Indian students?
**Fintiba** is the most popular for Indians due to its app, zero fees, and responsive support. **Expatrio** is fastest (3–5 days). Both are free and reliable—choose based on your timeline.
Can my parents' money go into the Sperrkonto, or does it have to be my own?
Yes, your parents' funds are fine. The account is in your name, but the source can be your family. Many Indian students fund it with parental bank transfers or education loans.
How long does it take to open a Sperrkonto?
Expatrio is fastest (3–5 days). Fintiba and Coracle take 1–3 weeks. Deutsche Bank may take 2–3 weeks. Plan to open the account 2–3 months before your visa interview.
Do I lose the money in the Sperrkonto, or can I withdraw it?
You can withdraw it—but only after arriving in Germany and registering. Once you have your residence permit, you receive monthly disbursements (usually EUR 992). After your studies end, the remaining balance is refunded.
What happens if I don't use all EUR 11,904 in one year?
If you leave Germany or your course ends before 12 months, the remaining balance is refunded to you (minus any fees). There's no penalty for having 'leftover' money.
Is a Sperrkonto required if I have a full scholarship?
No. If you've received a full scholarship (100%) covering tuition + living expenses, you don't need a Sperrkonto. You'll provide the scholarship letter to the embassy instead.
Can I open a Sperrkonto if I don't have an admission letter yet?
No. Most providers require an admission letter (acceptance or Zulassungsbescheid) to open the account, as it proves your enrollment. Apply for university admission first.
What if I need more than EUR 11,904 for 2 years or because of dependents?
For 2 years, double the amount: EUR 23,808. For a spouse or dependent, add another EUR 5,952 per person. Adjust your Sperrkonto deposit accordingly.
