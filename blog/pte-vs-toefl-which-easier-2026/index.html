PTE vs TOEFL 2026: Which Is Easier? Format, Scoring, AI Grading & Results

Torn between PTE and TOEFL? This guide compares both exams on format, difficulty, AI scoring, speaking quality, acceptance, and results speed to help you choose.

Written and reviewed by the LandingPrep editorial team · Last updated 2026-06-22 · Sources are linked inline and verified against official test-maker, university and government pages.

⚡ Quick answer: Both PTE (Pearson Test of English) and TOEFL (Test of English as a Foreign Language) are globally accepted English language exams for university admissions, work visas, and immigration. But they are **very different** in format, scoring, and difficulty. **PTE** is a **computer-based, AI-scored exam** with no human graders.

PTE vs TOEFL: Quick Comparison Overview

Both PTE (Pearson Test of English) and TOEFL (Test of English as a Foreign Language) are globally accepted English language exams for university admissions, work visas, and immigration. But they are very different in format, scoring, and difficulty.

PTE is a computer-based, AI-scored exam with no human graders. It tests all four skills (reading, writing, listening, speaking) in an integrated, task-based format. You complete it in one sitting (~3 hours) and receive results in 3–5 business days.

TOEFL (specifically the iBT—Internet-Based Test) is a computer-based exam with human scoring for writing and speaking. It separates reading, writing, listening, and speaking into distinct sections and takes 3 hours. Results arrive in 6–10 business days.

Which is easier depends on your strengths: if you speak naturally and prefer speed, PTE is your edge. If you're comfortable with structured templates and want human feedback, TOEFL may suit you better.

PTE Format & Structure

PTE Academic consists of three main sections that are completed in one sitting without breaks (except brief pauses between sections):

Section 1: Speaking & Writing (~77–93 minutes) - Personal introduction (no score impact, just to calibrate the AI) - Read aloud (speaking): You read a passage silently, then speak it aloud. AI scores fluency, pronunciation, and rhythm. - Repeat sentence (speaking): You hear a sentence, repeat it verbatim. Measures listening + speaking accuracy. - Describe image (speaking): You describe a chart or graph in 40 seconds. - Re-tell lecture (speaking): You hear a short lecture, summarize it in 40 seconds. - Answer short question (speaking): You answer 10–12 quick-fire questions ('What's the capital of France?' style). - Summary from lecture (writing): You hear a 60-second lecture, write a 50–75 word summary. - Essay (writing): You write a 200–300 word essay on a given topic (e.g., 'Should AI replace human teachers?') in 20 minutes.

Section 2: Reading (~32–40 minutes) - Multiple-choice, single answer - Multiple-choice, multiple answers - Re-order paragraphs (drag-and-drop to arrange a jumbled text) - Fill in the blanks (drag matching words into blanks in a passage) - Reading & writing fill-in-the-blanks (read, then type missing words)

Section 3: Listening (~45–57 minutes) - Summarize spoken text: Hear a passage (~60 seconds), write a 50–75 word summary. - Multiple-choice, single/multiple answers - Fill in the blanks (type missing words in a transcript as you listen) - Highlight correct summary (choose the best summary of a lecture) - Multiple-choice, multiple answers (choose all correct statements) - Select missing word (choose a word that fills a blank in a recording) - Write from dictation (type a sentence exactly as you hear it—spelling matters)

The critical difference: PTE is task-integrated. Reading and writing blend together. Speaking and listening intertwine. You never see a 'listening' section by itself—instead, you summarize lectures while listening, or repeat sentences, mixing skills throughout.

🔑
PTE favors non-native speakers because it values consistent pronunciation and grammar, not 'native-like' accent. AI doesn't judge accent—only clarity, stress, and rhythm.

TOEFL Format & Structure

TOEFL iBT (Internet-Based Test) has four clearly separated sections. You tackle them in this order:

Section 1: Reading (~54–72 minutes) - 3–4 passages (~650–750 words each) - 10 questions per passage (multiple-choice, single answer) - Question types: main idea, vocabulary in context, inference, detail, sentence insertion, prose summary - Each question is worth 1 point; passage is worth 10 points - Familiar topics: history, science, sociology, art

Section 2: Writing (~20–50 minutes) - Integrated Task (20 min): Read a passage (3 min), listen to a lecture on the same topic (2 min), then write a 150–225 word summary of how the lecture relates to the reading. - Independent Task (30 min): Write a 300+ word essay responding to a prompt ('Do you agree that online education is better than in-person learning?'). - Human raters score each task. Integrated task is scored 0–5; independent task is also 0–5.

Section 3: Listening (~41–57 minutes) - 3–4 lectures (~3–5 min each) - 6 questions per lecture - 2–3 conversations (~3 min each) - 5 questions per conversation - Question types: gist-content, gist-purpose, detail, function, attitude, organization, inference

Section 4: Speaking (~12–25 minutes) - Task 1 (Independent): Answer a personal question ('Describe a place you like to visit'). You have 45 seconds to respond after a 15-second prep time. - Task 2 (Integrated-Reading+Listening): Read a passage (70 seconds), listen to a lecture (~60–80 seconds), then speak for 60 seconds explaining the relationship. - Task 3 (Integrated-Listening): Listen to a student discussion (~90 seconds), then speak for 60 seconds summarizing the student's problem and explaining the two solutions they discussed. - Task 4 (Integrated-Listening): Listen to a lecture (~90 seconds) on a specific concept. Speak for 60 seconds explaining the concept using examples from the lecture.

Each speaking task is scored 0–4 by trained raters (and sometimes an automated speech-recognition system for backup). Your overall speaking score is the average.

Each reading passage question = 1 point. Total reading score = points earned / total points × 30. Similarly for listening (0–30 points). Writing (0–30 points: 15 per task, averaged). Speaking (0–30 points: averaged across 4 tasks).

⚠️
TOEFL's integrated writing and speaking tasks must follow a specific template structure. Raters expect you to paraphrase the source material, not quote it verbatim. Failing to follow the template = low score, even if your English is perfect.

Difficulty & Fairness: AI Scoring vs. Human Scoring

PTE: AI Scoring

PTE is scored 100% by Pearson's proprietary AI model (no human involvement). This sounds cold, but it has massive advantages:

1. Zero bias: The AI doesn't care about your accent, gender, or country. It measures pronunciation acoustically (pitch, energy, duration), not subjectively. A strong Indian English accent scores the same as an American accent—the AI only checks clarity. 2. Consistency: Every test-taker is scored by the same algorithm. No 'rater drift' (where one human grader is stricter than another). 3. Speed: Results in 3–5 days because there's no queue of human raters. 4. But: The AI can be nitpicky on grammar and vocabulary. A single word mispronounced or misspelled can cost points. It's unforgiving of natural speech patterns (like hesitations or filler words).

TOEFL: Human + AI Scoring

TOEFL is scored by trained human raters (and sometimes supported by an e-rater for writing). Advantages:

1. Context: Humans understand nuance. If you misspeak once but correct yourself, a human might ignore it. The AI does not. 2. Feedback loop: Some test centers provide score rationales. You know *why* you got a 4/4 on speaking—specific strengths noted. 3. Flexibility: Speaking with an accent? If you're clear, humans are forgiving. AI is less so. 4. But: Human raters fatigue. If you test on a Friday afternoon, you might get a stricter rater who's seen 50 tests already. Bias exists (even unintentional).

The Verdict: PTE's AI is fairer for non-natives because it strips accent and emotion out of scoring. TOEFL's humans are more forgiving if you show personality—but inconsistency is a risk.

💡
If you have a strong accent but excellent grammar and vocabulary, PTE rewards you. If you speak naturally with slight grammar errors but strong communication, TOEFL might be kinder.

Speaking Quality: Which Exam Suits Your Accent?

This is the single biggest difference for Indian students.

PTE Speaking Scoring: - The AI listens for: fluency (no long pauses), pronunciation (clarity of individual sounds), intonation (natural stress and rhythm), and grammar accuracy. - It does NOT care if you sound 'native.' A clear Indian English speaker scores as high as a British English speaker. - Hesitations, filler words ('um,' 'uh'), and self-corrections hurt your score because they indicate lack of fluency. The AI interprets them as low proficiency. - You must speak naturally but flawlessly. No stumbles allowed.

TOEFL Speaking Scoring: - Human raters use a 4-point scale per task: - Score 4 (Excellent): Clear pronunciation, natural pacing, varied vocabulary, complex grammar. Accent? Doesn't matter if you're intelligible. - Score 3 (Good): Mostly clear, occasional awkward phrasing, but meaning is always understood. - Score 2 (Fair): Pronunciation challenges, halting speech, but still understandable overall. - Score 1 (Weak): Hard to understand, lots of errors. - Humans are more forgiving of hesitations and self-corrections. If you pause to gather your thoughts, then deliver a strong response, you might still score 4. - Accent is irrelevant as long as you're intelligible. - Natural speech patterns (slight filler words, thinking pauses) are actually expected and not penalized.

Real Example: - PTE task: 'Describe a city.' You pause for 2 seconds to think, then say 'Mumbai is... it's very crowded, and it has, uh, lots of culture.' → Likely 60-68 (deducted for pause and 'uh'). - TOEFL task: Same situation. You pause 2 seconds (prep time is permitted), then answer: 'Mumbai is very crowded and has lots of culture.' → Likely 3/4 (not penalized for thinking; answer is clear).

Winner for Indian students: TOEFL is slightly more forgiving of natural speech patterns, but PTE doesn't penalize accent—only clarity.

  • If you speak clearly without hesitations, both exams reward you equally.
  • If you naturally pause to think or use filler words, TOEFL is more forgiving.
  • If your accent is thick but intelligible, PTE's AI actually treats you fairly—humans might be more judgmental.
  • If you have grammar errors but strong communication, TOEFL's humans recognize overall meaning better than PTE's AI.

Acceptance: Which Universities & Visas Accept PTE vs. TOEFL?

TOEFL Acceptance: - USA: Required by almost all universities (except a few waive it for high GRE scores). - UK: Accepted but many prefer IELTS. Universities in Russell Group (Cambridge, Oxford, LSE) accept TOEFL, but IELTS is still preferred. - Canada: Fully accepted for all universities and study permits. - Australia: Accepted by all universities; IELTS is slightly preferred but TOEFL is equally valid. - New Zealand: Accepted by all universities. - Visa agencies: Canada, USA, Australia, UK, NZ all accept TOEFL for visa applications. - Overall: TOEFL is the global gold standard. Essentially every country that requires an English test accepts it.

PTE Acceptance: - USA: Accepted by most universities, but TOEFL is still preferred. A few Ivy League schools (Harvard, Yale) quietly prefer TOEFL. - UK: Accepted by all universities under UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) rules, BUT only PTE Academic with a security certificate counts. Regular PTE Academic scores are NOT accepted for visa purposes. - Canada: Accepted for study permits and immigration (NOT the security-certificate variant; regular PTE is fine). - Australia: Accepted by all universities and for visas. - New Zealand: Accepted by universities, but IELTS is still preferred. - Visa agencies: Australia and Canada accept PTE. UK accepts only the PTE Academic with security certificate (more expensive, more rigorous). USA accepts PTE, but fewer universities actively recruit on it. - Overall: PTE is growing but still secondary. If your target is a specific university, verify they accept PTE on their website—don't assume.

Critical Note: PTE comes in three variants: 1. PTE Academic (the standard): Globally recognized. 2. PTE Academic with Security Certificate: For UK visa applications only. It's the same exam, but administered with extra proctoring and security checks. Costs extra (~₹1,000 more) and must be taken at approved centers. 3. PTE General: For lower proficiency levels; NOT accepted for university admission.

For Indian students planning to study abroad: If you haven't chosen a university yet, TOEFL is safer because acceptance is universal. If your target university accepts PTE, both are equally valid.

Check the university's English language requirements page—it will list which tests are accepted and minimum scores.

🔑
UK universities accept PTE Academic, but UK Home Office (for visa purposes) ONLY accepts PTE Academic with Security Certificate. Know the difference—wrong variant = visa rejection.

Results Speed & Retake Policy

PTE: Fast Results, Frequent Retakes - Results: 3–5 business days (sometimes as fast as 1 day, but officially 5 days max). - Retake policy: You can retake PTE every 5 calendar days. If you test on Monday, you can test again on Saturday of the same week. - Score validity: 2 years from test date. - Reporting: You can choose which scores to report to universities at the time of test registration—multiple attempts don't show up unless you list them. - Advantage: If you bomb the first attempt, you can recover fast. 3 attempts in 2 weeks is feasible. - Disadvantage: The quick turnaround can tempt you to retake without proper prep. Each PTE costs ~$160; retaking costs money and time.

TOEFL: Slower Results, Spaced Retakes - Results: 6–10 business days (officially up to 10 days, but usually 6–7). - Retake policy: You can retake TOEFL every 12 days. If you test on the 15th, you can test again on the 27th at the earliest. - Score validity: 2 years from test date. - Score choice: You can select which scores to send to universities after you see results (no penalty for multiple attempts shown). - Advantage: Forces you to prepare properly between attempts. Less temptation to blindly retake. - Disadvantage: If you're applying for a university with a tight deadline, waiting 12 days between retakes is risky.

Verdict: If you have a flexible timeline (3+ months), both are fine. If you're 2 months from the deadline, PTE's faster retake window is an edge. If you're under 1 month, neither exam is ideal—focus on nailing one attempt.

  1. Assess your starting level: Take a free PTE or TOEFL mock test online. Honest score tells you realistic improvement timeline.
  2. If you're 30+ points below target: You need 6–8 weeks of structured prep. Choose based on comfort (PTE for fast speaking, TOEFL for templates).
  3. If you're 15–20 points below: 3–4 weeks of targeted practice. PTE's faster retake allows two attempts in 10 days; TOEFL requires waiting.
  4. If you're already near target (5–10 points away): You can afford one or two retakes. Choose the exam that aligns with your speaking style.
  5. Register for test: Book 2–3 weeks out to allow focused prep. PTE centers are less common; confirm availability before registering.

Costs & Test Center Availability

PTE Academic Costs: - Exam fee: ~USD $160 (₹13,200 approx) per attempt. - Booking fee (if any): Usually included. - With security certificate (UK visa requirement): +USD $30–50 (~₹2,500–4,000). - Total for one attempt with full reporting: ~₹13,200–14,000. - Test centers in India: Limited. Major cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai) have centers, but not everywhere. Availability can be sparse (tests fill up fast). - Registration timeline: Book at least 2 weeks in advance for guaranteed slot.

TOEFL iBT Costs: - Exam fee: ~USD $210 (₹17,400 approx) per attempt. - Booking fee (if any): Usually included. - Rush services (priority scoring, extra reporting): +USD $15–25. - Total for one attempt: ~₹17,400–19,000. - Test centers in India: Widespread. ETS (Educational Testing Service, the TOEFL administrator) has centers in most cities and many smaller towns. Availability is generally better than PTE. - Registration timeline: Can sometimes book 1 week in advance; typically 2–3 weeks out.

True Cost of Multiple Attempts: - PTE: 2 attempts = ~₹26,400–28,000. 3 attempts = ~₹39,600–42,000. - TOEFL: 2 attempts = ~₹34,800–38,000. 3 attempts = ~₹52,200–57,000.

Financial verdict: PTE is ~₹4,000 cheaper per attempt. If you're budget-conscious and may retake, PTE is more economical. If you can afford it, TOEFL's wider availability means less hassle traveling to test centers.

Which Exam Should You Choose? Decision Framework

Here's a practical guide to choose based on your situation:

Choose PTE if: - You're confident in your spoken English and can speak fluently without hesitation. - You want results fast (3–5 days instead of 6–10). - You may need to retake; the 5-day retake window is crucial. - Your target universities accept PTE (confirmed on their website). - You're budget-conscious and may retake multiple times. - You're comfortable with AI evaluation and don't want human bias. - You have access to a PTE test center nearby (Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, etc.).

Choose TOEFL if: - You're strong at structure and templates; you prefer frameworks over free speaking. - You naturally pause to think, use filler words, or self-correct—humans will forgive this more than AI. - Your target is USA universities (TOEFL is the gold standard there). - You're aiming for highly selective universities (Ivy League, top 20 schools) that secretly prefer TOEFL. - You want flexibility in speaking: time to gather thoughts, natural pauses, etc. - You need a clear prep structure (TOEFL courses are abundant; PTE prep is less standardized). - You want speaking feedback (some test centers provide detailed speaking breakdowns). - Your nearest test center is not a major city; TOEFL has more centers.

Can't decide? Take a free PTE mock (12–15 min speaking section) and a TOEFL speaking task (online templates available). See which feels more natural. If the PTE speaking task feels rushed and unnatural, TOEFL is better for you. If the TOEFL template feels rigid and limiting, PTE is better.

ℹ️
Many Indian students who score 85+ on PTE could score 110+ on TOEFL—and vice versa. The exam choice can swing your score by 5–15 points. Choose wisely based on your strengths, not the 'easier' label.

Frequently asked questions

Is PTE really easier than TOEFL?
Not necessarily. PTE rewards fluent, error-free speech and is scored by AI (no bias). TOEFL rewards clear communication with forgiving humans. If you speak naturally with slight hesitations, TOEFL is 'easier.' If you're fluent and confident, both are equally easy.
Can I use the same score for both PTE and TOEFL applications?
No. They're separate exams with separate scores. A PTE 85 is not the same as a TOEFL 100. Universities have different minimums for each test. Check your target university's requirements.
How long are PTE and TOEFL scores valid?
Both are valid for **2 years** from the test date. After 2 years, you must retake the exam if you need to apply to another university.
Do all universities accept PTE?
Most universities accept PTE, but TOEFL is still the default. If you're applying to a highly selective US university, confirm they accept PTE on their website. Many top schools list TOEFL, IELTS, and PTE—but PTE is often the smallest category.
Which exam is better for UK visa applications?
If you're applying to a UK university, both PTE and TOEFL are accepted by the university itself. But for **UK Home Office** (visa), you need **PTE Academic with Security Certificate** or IELTS. Regular PTE scores won't work for UK visas. Check before registering.
Can I retake PTE and TOEFL in the same month?
Yes. PTE allows retakes every 5 days and TOEFL every 12 days. You could take PTE on the 1st, retake on the 6th, then take TOEFL on the 15th, and retake TOEFL on the 27th. But this is exhausting and expensive—focus on one exam.
What's the average score boost if I retake?
On average, Indian students improve **10–15 points on PTE** and **5–10 points on TOEFL** on their second attempt, assuming they address weak areas. Some improve 20+ points; others plateau. Quality prep between attempts is key.
If I score 85 on PTE, what's the equivalent TOEFL score?
PTE 85 is roughly equivalent to TOEFL 100–105. But equivalency charts are approximate. Focus on your target university's minimum, not the conversion.
Is AI scoring for PTE really fair?
Yes, for non-native speakers, PTE's AI is fairer than human raters. It doesn't judge accent—only clarity, pronunciation accuracy, fluency, and grammar. An Indian English speaker with perfect grammar scores the same as a British speaker with the same grammar. The risk: the AI is unforgiving of hesitations and filler words.
