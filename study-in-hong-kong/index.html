Study in Hong Kong 2026: The Complete Guide for International Students

Hong Kong pairs world-top-50, English-medium universities with the most generous stay-back scheme in Asia: the IANG arrangement gives graduates 24 months to stay — with no job offer required for recent graduates — and your study years count toward the 7-year path to permanent residence. This guide walks the whole journey: universities, admission, costs, the student visa, IANG, and settling long-term.

⚡ Quick answer: Hong Kong's universities are English-medium and globally ranked (HKU #17, CUHK #36, HKUST #47); tuition is ≈ HKD 140,000–210,000/yr (US$18–27k); the main intake is September; and after graduating the IANG scheme gives 24 months to stay with no job offer needed — with study years counting toward the 7-year PR path.

Why study in Hong Kong?

  • World-top-50, English-medium universities. HKU (#17), CUHK (#36) and HKUST (#47) teach in English and rank among the world's best. See all top universities in Hong Kong.
  • The standout stay-back: the IANG scheme gives graduates a 24-month stay with no job offer required for recent graduates — rare in Asia.
  • Gateway to Greater China & a global finance/tech hub — strong for business, finance, computer science and data.
  • Real PR route: seven years of ordinary residence (your study years count) leads to the right of abode.

What it costs

Tuition for non-local students is roughly HKD 140,000–210,000 per year (about US$18,000–27,000) — meaningfully lower than comparable US or UK programmes for a similar global ranking. Living costs add about HKD 60,000–120,000 per year, with accommodation the biggest expense (Hong Kong housing is famously pricey, and on-campus places are limited). Admission & requirements

  • Academic: a recognised bachelor's degree with a strong record for Master's entry; top programmes are competitive.
  • English proficiency: typically IELTS 6.0–6.5 or TOEFL ~79–90. Check the exact bar and practise with IELTS for HKU, TOEFL for HKU, and a free IELTS mock test or TOEFL mock test.
  • GRE/GMAT: often recommended for competitive business and analytics Master's (GMAT ~650–680 range at the top schools).
  • Applications go directly to each university, usually in rounds — apply early for the best admission and scholarship odds.

The student visa

Once admitted, your university sponsors your student visa application to the Hong Kong Immigration Department. You'll need your admission/enrolment proof, financial evidence covering tuition and living costs, and a valid passport. Start as soon as you have your offer, as processing takes several weeks. Most non-local students may take on-campus part-time work and study-related internships — always confirm the current conditions attached to your visa (the "No Limit of Stay"/student conditions) before working.

After graduation: IANG & the 7-year PR path

  • IANG — 24-month stay, no job offer needed: under the Immigration Arrangements for Non-local Graduates, graduates are normally granted an initial 24-month stay. Recent graduates — those applying within six months of graduating — do not need a job offer to apply.
  • Renew & extend: once you're working you can renew your IANG status and keep building residence.
  • Permanent residence at 7 years: after seven years of continuous ordinary residence — and your study years count — you can apply for the right of abode (PR).

Immigration rules change — always confirm the current IANG and right-of-abode details on the official Hong Kong Immigration Department ↗ before you rely on them.

Scholarships & funding

Hong Kong universities offer entrance and merit scholarships for strong international applicants, plus Belt and Road scholarships for students from participating regions and the highly competitive Hong Kong PhD Fellowship Scheme (HKPFS) for research students. Frequently asked questions

Is Hong Kong good for international students?
Yes, especially if you want a world-ranked, English-medium education in Asia with a strong stay-back option. Universities like HKU, HKUST and CUHK rank in the global top 50, teach in English, and sit in a major finance and tech hub. The standout benefit is the IANG scheme, which lets graduates stay 24 months after finishing — with no job offer required for recent graduates.
How much does it cost to study in Hong Kong?
Tuition for non-local students is roughly HKD 140,000–210,000 per year (about US$18,000–27,000), which is lower than comparable programmes in the US or UK. Living costs add about HKD 60,000–120,000 per year, with accommodation the biggest and most expensive factor. Budget carefully for housing, especially near campus.
Can I study in Hong Kong in English?
Yes. English is the medium of instruction at most Hong Kong universities, particularly for postgraduate programmes, so you do not need Cantonese or Mandarin to study there. A typical English requirement is IELTS 6.0–6.5 or TOEFL ~79–90, depending on the university and programme.
Can I stay and work in Hong Kong after I graduate?
Yes — this is Hong Kong's biggest draw. Under the Immigration Arrangements for Non-local Graduates (IANG), graduates are normally granted an initial stay of 24 months, and recent graduates (applying within six months of graduating) do NOT need a job offer to apply. If you find work you can renew and extend your stay, making it one of Asia's most graduate-friendly stay-back schemes.
Can I get permanent residency in Hong Kong?
Yes, through the 7-year rule: after seven years of continuous ordinary residence you can apply for the right of abode (permanent residence). Importantly, your years spent studying in Hong Kong count toward those seven years, so studying there is a genuine long-term settlement route — one of the clearer PR paths in Asia.
When should I apply to study in Hong Kong?
The main intake is September (Autumn). Postgraduate applications typically open around September–November of the year before and are assessed in rounds through to spring, so applying early improves both your admission and scholarship chances. Allow time afterwards for the university-sponsored student visa.
