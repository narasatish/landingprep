ACT Math: Content & Calculator Strategy

What ACT Math covers, why a calculator is allowed throughout, and how to keep pace on a fast section.

The big picture

  • ACT Math (1–36)
    • Pre-Algebra & Algebra — Numbers, equations, functions
    • Geometry — Lines, angles, area, volume, coordinates
    • Trigonometry — Right triangles, basic trig ratios
    • Calculator allowed — On every Math question

School-level content, broad coverage

ACT Math spans pre-algebra and algebra, geometry, and some trigonometry — all standard high-school topics. It's broad rather than deep: many topics, mostly straightforward, so gaps in any one area cost points across the section.

💡
Real example: You'll see everything from percentages and linear equations to the area of a circle and a basic sine ratio — reviewing your weak topics matters more than mastering advanced tricks.
🧠 Memory hook: Broad high-school maths: algebra, geometry, a little trig. Fix your weak topics.

A calculator is allowed throughout

You can use an approved calculator on every ACT Math question — but many are faster by reasoning or estimation. Use the calculator for real computation, not for problems you can set up and solve in your head.

💡
Real example: For '15% of 240', mental math (10% = 24, plus half = 36) is faster than typing; save the calculator for messy arithmetic and graphing.
🧠 Memory hook: Calculator on every question — but reason first when it's quicker than typing.

Answer choices are a tool

Since questions are multiple choice, use the options: plug in the answer choices (start with a middle value) or plug in your own numbers for variable problems. This turns hard algebra into quick checking.

💡
Real example: 'For which value of x is the equation true?' Test the middle option first — too big or small tells you which way to go, so you rarely test more than two.
🧠 Memory hook: Use the options: back-solve from the middle, or plug in your own numbers.

Pace and never leave a blank

Math is timed tightly, so keep moving — do the quick questions first, flag the long ones, and return. With no penalty for wrong answers, put an answer on every question before time runs out.

💡
Real example: A multi-step geometry problem eating two minutes? Flag it, bubble a guess, and move to easier points — come back if time allows.
🧠 Memory hook: Quick ones first, flag the long ones. No penalty — bubble every question.

Frequently asked questions

What content does ACT Math cover?
Pre-algebra and algebra, geometry, and some trigonometry — standard high-school topics, broad rather than deep.
Is a calculator allowed on ACT Math?
Yes — an approved calculator can be used on every Math question, though many are faster by reasoning.
How can you use the multiple-choice format to your advantage?
Plug in the answer choices (starting from the middle) or plug in your own numbers for variable problems.
Why should you flag long questions?
Math is tightly timed, so banking quick points first and returning to long questions later protects your score.
Should you ever leave an ACT Math question blank?
No — there's no penalty for wrong answers, so guess on every question you can't finish.
